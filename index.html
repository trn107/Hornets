Know The Game

Rules

Two calls every new hockey parent hears a lot from the stands — here's what they actually mean.

Offsides Attacking players have to let the puck cross the blue line before they do. +

Attacking players have to let the puck cross the blue line into the offensive zone before they do. If a player's skates cross the line ahead of the puck, the referee blows the whistle for offside and play stops for a faceoff outside the zone — it stops players from camping out by the other team's net waiting for a breakaway pass.

Differences between NHL and 12U:

NHL

Offsides is Delayed Offsides, where the whistle is only blown once an attacking player touches the puck over the Blue Line. Offsides in this case is waved off if the attackers exit the zone then re-enter before touching the puck.

12U

Offsides is called whether an attacker touches the puck or not, anytime the puck enters the zone after an attacking player has entered the zone first.

Icing Shooting the puck the length of the ice untouched stops play and comes right back. +

If a player shoots the puck from their own half of the ice all the way across the opponent's goal line without anyone touching it, the whistle blows for icing. Play stops and comes right back for a faceoff in the icing team's own zone — so you can't just fire the puck down the ice to escape pressure or kill time.

Differences between NHL and 12U:

NHL

There is no Icing if the offending team (committing the Icing) is on a penalty kill. Also, when Icing is called, the offending team is not allowed to change players — this is to prevent teams from icing the puck in a difficult situation just to change players.

12U

Icing is still called when a team is on the penalty kill. Teams are allowed to change players, so it can sometimes be useful to commit an icing on purpose to change players.
