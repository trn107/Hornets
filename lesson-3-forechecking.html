Learning Center · Lesson 3

Forechecking

Once the puck is in the other team's end, a Hornet's job is to make it as hard as possible for them to skate it back out clean.

Forechecking is the pressure a team applies in the offensive zone right after the puck goes in deep — whether it was dumped in on purpose or the other team just turned it over. Done well, it's not a mad scramble toward the puck. Every player has a lane to cover and a job to do, so the other team never gets the clean, easy breakout they want.

F1 (Dog) is the first forward in the zone. F1 goes straight to the puck carrier and tries to force them to the wall by coming in at an angle. F2 (Fox), the second forward to enter the zone, goes to take away the wing on the strong side wall (the side where the puck is) to make sure he can't take a pass from the D. F3 (Hawk) is the third forward in the zone. This forward stays a bit higher toward the middle of the zone. F3 (Hawk) is in a better position to come back to help the defense if the other team is successful in breaking out.

Key Coaching Points

  • 1The first forechecker attacks from an angle, not straight on — take away one side of the ice and force a decision.
  • 2The second forechecker reads the first — fill the passing lane the first forechecker didn't take away.
  • 3Support each other's coverage. If multiple forecheckers chase the puck, someone's left wide open.
  • 4Stick in the lane beats legs to the puck — cut off the pass before you worry about winning a race.
  • 5Lost the race? Get back into position instead of chasing — a bad read is worse than no forecheck at all.
