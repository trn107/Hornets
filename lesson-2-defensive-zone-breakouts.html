Defensive Zone Breakouts

Once a Hornet comes up with the puck in their own end, the job flips: from defending the house to getting the puck out safely, as a team, with speed.

A breakout is just what it sounds like — breaking the puck out of your own zone. The team that does it calmly, with everyone in a predictable spot, turns defense into offense in a few seconds. The team that panics and rims the puck blindly usually just hands it right back. Every Hornet on the ice has a job during a breakout, even the players who never touch the puck.

D1 moves to retrieve the puck. D2 watches the front of the net. The wing on the side with the puck goes to the wall. The wing on the other side goes to the middle or goes to the far wall. D1, with the puck, first looks to the strong side wing on the wall (the side that the puck is on) for a pass. The wing takes the pass and looks to skate out of the zone or hit the center. The center comes down and loops around to help support the breakout, pick up speed, and possibly take an outlet pass from the wing.

Key Coaching Points

  • 1Know where the forecheck is before you touch the puck — a quick look over both shoulders behind your own net saves a turnover.
  • 2First option is the strong-side winger along the boards — it's the fastest, safest way out of the zone.
  • 3Center swings low into the middle of the ice as the safety valve if the wall pass isn't there.
  • 4The defenseman without the puck opens up along the blue line — not straight up the middle of the ice.
  • 5Nothing open? Rim it hard around the boards and reset. A clean stoppage beats forcing a pass through traffic in front of your own net.
